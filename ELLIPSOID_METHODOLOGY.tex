\title{Mahalanobis Ellipsoidal Steering\\
\large Methodology and Qwen2.5-3B TruthfulQA Protocol}
\author{}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We describe a Mahalanobis ellipsoidal extension of Spherical Steering for
inference-time control of language-model hidden states.  The method estimates
the center and covariance geometry of calibration activations, applies a
scale-normalized fractional whitening map, performs a norm-preserving
spherical rotation in transformed coordinates, and maps the result back to
the original representation space. We present
diagonal and low-rank-plus-isotropic covariance representations, a stable
matrix-free intervention, leakage-safe nested evaluation on TruthfulQA, and
the validation procedure used for Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct. The construction
preserves a powered-Mahalanobis transformed radius (the conventional
Mahalanobis radius when $p=1/2$). This ellipsoidal method
is an extension of, rather than a method evaluated in, the original Spherical
Steering paper.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation and Scope}

Ordinary activation addition changes both the direction and magnitude of a
hidden representation.  Spherical Steering instead rotates a hidden state on
a Euclidean sphere and preserves its Euclidean norm.  The spherical model,
however, treats the origin as the center of the representation geometry and
all directions as having equal scale.  Transformer activations can be
nonzero-centered, anisotropic, and strongly correlated.  We therefore replace
the Euclidean geometry by a constant Mahalanobis metric estimated from
calibration activations.

Let $\bm h\in\R^d$ be a hidden state, $\bm c\in\R^d$ a calibration center, and
$\bm\Sigma\succ0$ a regularized covariance estimator.  Define
\begin{equation}
  \bm z = \bm W_p(\bm h-\bm c),
  \qquad \bm W_p=\tau^p\bm\Sigma^{-p},\quad
  \tau=\operatorname{tr}(\bm\Sigma)/d,\quad 0\leq p\leq\tfrac12.
\end{equation}
We rotate $\bm z$ toward a truthful prototype in transformed coordinates and map
the result back using $\bm W_p^{-1}$. At $p=0$ this is the identity geometry;
at $p=1/2$ it is normalized full whitening. The trace normalization makes the
map invariant to global covariance scale. The trajectory is a spherical geodesic in
transformed coordinates, equivalently a geodesic under a powered Mahalanobis
metric.  We do \emph{not} claim that it is an ambient-Euclidean geodesic of an
embedded ellipsoid.

\section{Calibration Data and Leakage Control}

For every TruthfulQA question--answer candidate, we concatenate the exact
evaluation prompt and candidate token sequences and extract every hidden
position whose next-token logits score the answer. For prompt length $P$ and
answer length $L$, these are $P-1,\ldots,P+L-2$. Each receives weight $1/L$,
so every answer candidate contributes unit total fitting weight. Each
activation is paired with a binary label $y_i\in\{0,1\}$, where
$1$ denotes a truthful answer and $0$ an incorrect or hallucinated answer.
Activations belonging to the same question are always assigned to the same
split.

For each outer fold, let $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{fit}}$ be the prototype-fitting
set, $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{val}}$ the hyperparameter-validation set, and
$\mathcal D_{\mathrm{test}}$ the untouched test set.  All quantities below---the
center, class means, covariance axes, shrinkage target, retained rank,
prototypes, and radius quantiles---are estimated exclusively from
$\mathcal D_{\mathrm{fit}}$.  Validation and test activations never participate
in geometry fitting.

Our adapted Qwen2.5-3B protocol uses two randomized outer folds with seed 42.
Within each outer development fold, 20\% of questions are reserved for
validation and the remaining 80\% are used to fit prototypes.  Hyperparameters
are selected on validation data and frozen before the outer test fold is
evaluated.  The process is repeated independently for the second outer fold.

\section{Ellipsoid Estimation}

Given fitting activations $\bm x_i$ and reliability weights $w_i$, define the
weighted class means $\bm m_T,\bm m_H$. We independently sweep three steering
origins,
\begin{equation}
 \bm c_{\rm zero}=\bm0,\qquad
 \bm c_{\rm global}=\frac{\sum_iw_i\bm x_i}{\sum_iw_i},\qquad
 \bm c_{\rm midpoint}=\tfrac12(\bm m_T+\bm m_H).
\end{equation}
Weighted covariance uses denominator
$D_w=\sum_iw_i-(\sum_iw_i^2)/(\sum_iw_i)$. For reference, with uniform
weights the means reduce to
\begin{align}
  \bm m_T &= \frac{1}{n_T}\sum_{i:y_i=1}\bm x_i, &
  \bm m_H &= \frac{1}{n_H}\sum_{i:y_i=0}\bm x_i,\\
  \bm c &= \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bm x_i.
\end{align}

\subsection{Covariance residuals}

We support two covariance sources.  Global covariance uses
\begin{equation}
  \bm r_i=\bm x_i-\bm c.
\end{equation}
Pooled within-class covariance uses
\begin{equation}
  \bm r_i =
  \begin{cases}
    \bm x_i-\bm m_T,&y_i=1,\\
    \bm x_i-\bm m_H,&y_i=0.
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
We recommend pooled residuals because the truthful--hallucinated mean
difference does not itself inflate a covariance axis.

\subsection{Shrinkage and flooring}

Let $v_j$ denote an estimated diagonal variance or covariance eigenvalue and
let
\begin{equation}
  \tau=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}v_j.
\end{equation}
With shrinkage coefficient $\gamma\in[0,1]$, we use
\begin{equation}
  v_j^{(\gamma)}=(1-\gamma)v_j+\gamma\tau,
\end{equation}
followed by the relative and absolute floor
\begin{equation}
  v_j\leftarrow\max\{v_j,\epsilon\tau,\epsilon\},
  \qquad \epsilon>0.
\end{equation}
Covariance fitting is performed in float64 where feasible, and saved artifacts
use float32.

\subsection{Diagonal representation}

The diagonal estimator stores only
\begin{equation}
  \bm c\in\R^d,
  \qquad \bm v=\operatorname{diag}(\bm\Sigma)\in\R^d.
\end{equation}
Powered whitening and coloring are elementwise:
\begin{equation}
  \bm W_p\bm u=\bm u\odot(\bm v/\tau)^{-p},
  \qquad
  \bm W_p^{-1}\bm u=\bm u\odot(\bm v/\tau)^p.
\end{equation}
This is the lowest-memory estimator, but it ignores correlations between hidden
dimensions.

\subsection{Low-rank plus isotropic residual representation}

The low-rank estimator uses randomized truncated SVD of the fitting residual
matrix and never constructs a dense $d\times d$ covariance or whitening
matrix.  It represents
\begin{equation}
 \bm\Sigma=\bm U\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_k)\bm U\trans
 +\sigma_\perp^2(\bm I-\bm U\bm U\trans),
\end{equation}
where $\bm U\in\R^{d\times k}$ has orthonormal columns.  Before shrinkage, the
residual variance is estimated by
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_\perp^2=
  \frac{\operatorname{tr}(\widehat{\bm\Sigma})-
  \sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_j}{d-k}.
\end{equation}
Let $a_j=(\lambda_j/\tau)^{-p}$ and
$a_\perp=(\sigma_\perp^2/\tau)^{-p}$. The matrix-free powered whitening
operation is
\begin{equation}
\bm W_p\bm u = a_\perp\bm u+
\bm U\left[
\left(\bm a-a_\perp\bm 1\right)
\odot(\bm U\trans\bm u)
\right],
\end{equation}
and inverse coloring uses the same expression with powers $+p$:
\begin{equation}
\bm W_p^{-1}\bm u = (\sigma_\perp^2/\tau)^p\bm u+
\bm U\left[
\left((\bm\lambda/\tau)^p-(\sigma_\perp^2/\tau)^p\bm 1\right)
\odot(\bm U\trans\bm u)
\right].
\end{equation}

\section{Whitened Contrastive Prototypes}

The raw contrastive mean direction is
\begin{equation}
  \bm d=\bm m_T-\bm m_H.
\end{equation}
For ellipsoidal steering, it is essential to whiten this direction before
normalization:
\begin{equation}
  \bm d_z=\bm W_p\bm d,
  \qquad
  \mTz=\frac{\bm d_z}{\norm{\bm d_z}},
  \qquad
  \mHz=-\mTz.
\end{equation}
Fitting fails explicitly if $\norm{\bm d_z}$ is numerically negligible.  Raw
space spherical prototypes are not reused for ellipsoidal steering.

\section{Adaptive Mahalanobis Steering}

For a hidden state $\bm h$, compute
\begin{equation}
  \bm z=\bm W(\bm h-\bm c),
  \qquad r_M=\norm{\bm z},
  \qquad \widehat{\bm z}=\bm z/r_M.
\end{equation}
Near-zero $r_M$ produces an exact no-op.  Otherwise, calculate directional
similarities
\begin{equation}
  s_T=\widehat{\bm z}\trans\mTz,
  \qquad s_H=\widehat{\bm z}\trans\mHz,
\end{equation}
and vMF-style probabilities
\begin{equation}
  (p_T,p_H)=\operatorname{softmax}(\kappa s_T,\kappa s_H).
\end{equation}
Let $\delta=p_H-p_T$.  Steering triggers only when $\delta>\beta$, with
adaptive strength
\begin{equation}
  t=\operatorname{clip}\left(
  \alpha\frac{\delta-\beta}{1-\beta},0,1\right).
\end{equation}
Here $\kappa$ controls confidence sharpness, $\beta$ controls selectivity, and
$\alpha$ controls the maximum rotation strength.

\subsection{Stable exponential-map Slerp}

Let
\begin{equation}
 q=\operatorname{clip}(\widehat{\bm z}\trans\mTz,-1,1),
 \qquad \theta=\arccos q,
\end{equation}
and define the normalized tangent
\begin{equation}
  \bm v=\frac{\mTz-q\widehat{\bm z}}
  {\norm{\mTz-q\widehat{\bm z}}}.
\end{equation}
The rotated unit direction is
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\bm z}'=cos(t\theta)\widehat{\bm z}
  +\sin(t\theta)\bm v.
\end{equation}
For nearly antipodal vectors or a tiny tangent norm, a deterministic orthogonal
tangent is constructed from the coordinate basis vector least aligned with
$\widehat{\bm z}$.  Already-aligned vectors, invalid parameters, and nonfinite
inputs are handled explicitly.

Finally,
\begin{equation}
  \bm z'=r_M\widehat{\bm z}',
  \qquad
  \bm h'=\bm c+\bm W^{-1}\bm z'.
\end{equation}
Intervention arithmetic uses float32 and the output is returned in the input
activation dtype.

\section{Mahalanobis-Radius Invariance}

Because Slerp preserves unit norm and we restore the original transformed
radius $r_p$,
\begin{align}
  \norm{\bm W_p(\bm h'-\bm c)}^2
  &=\norm{\bm z'}^2\\
  &=r_p^2\\
  &=\norm{\bm W_p(\bm h-\bm c)}^2.
\end{align}
At $p=1/2$ this is, up to the common trace scale, the conventional
Mahalanobis-radius invariant
\begin{equation}
 (\bm h'-\bm c)\trans\bm\Sigma^{-1}(\bm h'-\bm c)
 =
 (\bm h-\bm c)\trans\bm\Sigma^{-1}(\bm h-\bm c),
\end{equation}
up to floating-point tolerance.  Observed maximum relative radius errors in
our experiments were approximately $10^{-7}$.

\section{TruthfulQA Evaluation Protocol}

We use Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct and matched scored-token activations. This retains the paper's
evaluation structure while changing the model size; consequently, it is an
adapted replication and cannot be expected to reproduce the paper's published
7B/8B numbers.

For multiple-choice evaluation, every truthful and incorrect reference answer
is scored by its conditional token log-likelihood.  MC1 indicates whether the
designated best truthful answer outranks all incorrect answers.  MC2 is the
normalized probability mass assigned to truthful answers.  MC3 is the fraction
of truthful references that outrank the highest-scoring incorrect reference.
We also report
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{MC\ Avg.}=\frac{\mathrm{MC1}+\mathrm{MC2}+\mathrm{MC3}}{3}.
\end{equation}
MC Avg. is a convenience summary used in this study; it is not one of the
three official TruthfulQA multiple-choice metrics and does not replace their
separate reporting.

\subsection{Evaluation metrics in detail}

Let $s(a\mid q)$ be the conditional log-likelihood of candidate answer $a$
under question prompt $q$. For an answer tokenization
$a=(a_1,\ldots,a_L)$,
\begin{equation}
 s(a\mid q)=\sum_{t=1}^{L}\log p(a_t\mid q,a_{<t}).
\end{equation}
Thus evaluation uses the sum, rather than the mean, of answer-token log
probabilities. Let $\mathcal A_T$ and $\mathcal A_F$ be the truthful and
incorrect reference-answer sets, and let $a^\star$ denote TruthfulQA's
designated best truthful answer. The per-question MC1 score is
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{MC1}(q)=
 \mathbb I\!\left[s(a^\star\mid q)>
 \max_{b\in\mathcal A_F}s(b\mid q)\right].
\end{equation}
MC2 exponentiates and normalizes the candidate scores and reports the total
probability assigned to truthful references:
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{MC2}(q)=
 \frac{\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_T}\exp s(a\mid q)}
 {\sum_{c\in\mathcal A_T\cup\mathcal A_F}\exp s(c\mid q)}.
\end{equation}
For numerical stability, the implementation may subtract the maximum
candidate score before exponentiation without changing the ratio. MC3 is
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{MC3}(q)=\frac{1}{|\mathcal A_T|}
 \sum_{a\in\mathcal A_T}
 \mathbb I\!\left[s(a\mid q)>
 \max_{b\in\mathcal A_F}s(b\mid q)\right].
\end{equation}
Dataset-level MC1, MC2, and MC3 are the respective means across questions.

The paper also reports open-ended generation metrics produced by two
pre-trained judge models: TRUE (\%) measures truthfulness and INFO (\%)
measures informativeness. Following prior work, their product
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{TRUE}\!\times\!\mathrm{INFO}
 =\frac{\mathrm{TRUE}\cdot\mathrm{INFO}}{100}\quad(\%)
\end{equation}
is the primary open-ended metric when TRUE and INFO are expressed as
percentages. The powered-ellipsoid findings reported in this document are
currently multiple-choice results only. A complete paper-style comparison
must additionally run the frozen configuration on open-ended generation and
report TRUE, INFO, and TRUE$\times$INFO without tuning on test judgments.

For the paper's other multiple-choice benchmarks---COPA, StoryCloze, BoolQ,
MMLU, and WinoGrande---each candidate option is scored by the same summed
conditional log-likelihood under its task prompt. The option with the highest
score is selected and benchmark accuracy is reported. Those benchmarks are
outside the present TruthfulQA experiment and should not be inferred from the
MC1/MC2/MC3 results.

For the MMLU experiment, the 57 subjects are grouped into STEM, Humanities,
Social Sciences, and Other.  Within each category, a deterministic shuffle
with seed 42 assigns the first 500 questions to development and the next 200
questions to frozen evaluation.  The 500 development questions are split
independently within category in an $80:20$ ratio, yielding 400 fitting and
100 validation questions per category.  Consequently, the balanced aggregate
contains 1,600 fitting, 400 validation, and 800 evaluation questions.  All
four candidate answers are represented during geometry fitting.  Activations
are collected at exactly the candidate-token positions whose summed
conditional log-likelihood determines the multiple-choice prediction, with
each answer receiving unit total weight irrespective of token length.  Center,
covariance, rank, whitening power, and steering hyperparameters are selected
only on the 400-question validation set; category and aggregate accuracies are
reported once on the disjoint 800-question evaluation set.

For COPA, the official 400-example training split is shuffled with seed 42 and
divided question-wise in a $4:1$ ratio: 320 examples fit the geometry and 80
form development validation.  The separate official 100-example validation
split is not consulted during fitting or selection and serves as the final test
set.  Each premise contributes its two candidate alternatives, and calibration
uses the same candidate-token activations whose summed conditional
log-likelihood determines test accuracy.  Geometry and steering settings are
frozen after validation before the single 100-example test evaluation.

For BoolQ, 1,000 examples are sampled after shuffling the official training
split with seed 42.  A question-wise $4:1$ division assigns 800 examples to
geometry fitting and 200 to development validation.  The full official
validation split of 3,270 examples is reserved for a single final evaluation.
Both candidate answers (\texttt{no} and \texttt{yes}) are represented using
answer-conditioned last-token activations. Unlike the multi-token benchmarks,
matched pre-answer scored-token calibration is degenerate here: both one-token
answers are predicted from the same prompt state, so their contrast is exactly
zero. The answer-conditioned policy is therefore recorded explicitly in the
artifact metadata. All geometry and intervention hyperparameters are selected
exclusively using the 200-example validation subset.

For StoryCloze, the complete provided English training partition forms the
development pool and is split question-wise in a $4:1$ fitting-to-validation
ratio using seed 42. The provided evaluation partition of 1,511 examples is
left untouched for the final evaluation. Both candidate endings are included
during calibration at the same candidate-token positions used by summed
conditional log-likelihood scoring. Geometry and steering configuration are
selected only on the development-validation subset and frozen before the
official evaluation run.

Each outer fold first assigns approximately half of the 817 questions to an
untouched test set. Twenty percent of the remaining development half is used
for validation and the other eighty percent for fitting. Thus the effective
fit:validation:test allocation is approximately $40:10:50$ (326--327,
81--82, and 408--409 questions). The fitting subset alone estimates centers,
class means, covariance spectra, and prototypes. The validation subset ranks
configurations; it is never used to refit them. The frozen winner is finally
evaluated once on the test subset.

With $\kappa=20$, $\alpha=0.8$, and $\beta=-0.8$ fixed, the staged tuning order is:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item at $p=0.5$, rank 128, and shrinkage 0.1, compare zero, global,
        and class-midpoint centers (three evaluations);
  \item holding the selected center fixed, compare
        $p\in\{0,0.125,0.25,0.375,0.5\}$ (five evaluations);
  \item holding the selected center and power fixed, compare ranks
        $\{32,64,128,256\}$ crossed with shrinkage
        $\{0.1,0.3,0.5,0.7\}$ (sixteen evaluations);
  \item freeze the best of the 24 validation evaluations and execute one test
        evaluation manually.
\end{enumerate}
The single-split selection score is
\begin{equation}
 J=\mathrm{MC2}-0.25\left|\rho_{\rm trigger}-0.9\right|.
\end{equation}
Raw spectra are cached and reused across compatible configurations. Invalid
ranks are skipped, completed configuration hashes can be resumed, and the
sweep code never invokes the test split.
The second outer fold repeats the complete selection process independently.
No parameter is changed after observing test results.

\section{Matched-Token Powered-Ellipsoid Findings}

The staged fold-0 sweep selected class-midpoint centering, partial whitening
$p=0.25$, rank 32, pooled within-class covariance, and shrinkage 0.1. This is
not full Mahalanobis whitening: it retains more of the original spherical
geometry while correcting the dominant correlated directions. The low rank
also indicates that the useful anisotropic correction is concentrated in a
small subspace rather than across all hidden dimensions.

\section{WinoGrande Transfer Experiment}

We additionally evaluate Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct on WinoGrande. Following the
paper's data protocol, we randomly sample 1,000 questions from the official
training split with seed 42, use 800 questions for fitting, and reserve 200 for
development validation. The full official validation split of 1,267 questions
is used for final accuracy evaluation. Each question has two candidate entity
fillers, and candidates are ranked by summed conditional token
log-likelihood.

The matched-token variant constructs exactly the prompt used during
evaluation, appends each candidate, and extracts hidden positions
$P-1,\ldots,P+L-2$. Each of the $L$ candidate tokens receives weight $1/L$.
The staged geometry search again compares three centers, five whitening
powers, and sixteen rank--shrinkage combinations using only the 200-question
development-validation subset.

Table~\ref{tab:wino-results} summarizes the observed official-validation
results. The unsteered baseline is independent of the calibration artifact.
The earlier spherical and ellipsoidal runs use last-token calibration, whereas
the final row uses matched scored-token calibration.

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\caption{Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct WinoGrande official-validation accuracy.}
\label{tab:wino-results}
\begin{tabular}{llrr}
\toprule
Method & Calibration & Trigger & Accuracy\\
\midrule
Unsteered & none & 0.0\% & 57.30\%\\
Sphere ($\alpha=.9,\beta=-.8$) & last token & 83.5\% & 56.27\%\\
Ellipsoid ($\alpha=.8,\beta=-.8$) & last token & 89.1\% & \textbf{58.48\%}\\
Ellipsoid ($\alpha=.3,\beta=-.8$) & matched tokens & 92.4\% & 56.99\%\\
\bottomrule

The matched-token sweep selected global centering, $p=0.25$, rank 256,
shrinkage 0.3, $\alpha=0.3$, and $\beta=-0.8$. It reached 66.0\% development-
validation accuracy with a 91.0\% token-level trigger rate, but fell to 56.99\%
on the official split while triggering 92.4\% of scored positions. The
9.01-point accuracy drop despite stable gating indicates validation overfit,
not a shift in gate frequency. It finished 0.31 points below the unsteered
baseline and 1.49 points below the earlier last-token ellipsoid.

The last-token ellipsoid improved over the unsteered baseline by 1.18 points
(approximately 15 additional correct questions), while the fixed spherical
configuration reduced accuracy by 1.03 points. For context, the paper reports
50.81\% baseline and 52.72\% spherical accuracy for LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct;
this is not a direct comparison because the model family and size differ.
Unlike the TruthfulQA result, matched-token calibration therefore did not
transfer successfully to WinoGrande under this split and search space.

These WinoGrande findings are exploratory. The official split was observed
before the final matched-token configuration was evaluated, so later runs are
post-hoc rather than pristine held-out tests. Confirmatory evidence would
require a new untouched split or a preregistered replication.

\section{Interpretation and Limitations}

The diagonal estimator is inexpensive but assumes coordinate-wise independence.
At Qwen layer 19 the earlier diagonal estimator produced a substantially lower
trigger rate and weaker validation metrics than correlated low-rank models.
Under matched-token fitting, the selected rank-32, $p=0.25$ representation
outperformed the earlier spherical fold-0 reference on all three MC metrics.
This supports partial, low-dimensional anisotropy correction for this setting;
it is not evidence that the same center, power, or rank will dominate on fold
1, other layers, or other models.

Multiple-choice tuning alone can favor aggressive rotations that harm
open-ended informativeness.  A complete study should additionally evaluate
TruthfulQA TRUE, INFO, and TRUE$\times$INFO on validation data before freezing
hyperparameters.  It should report both outer folds, uncertainty across seeds,
the sensitivity to layer and covariance rank, and a baseline using identical
prompts and splits.  Finally, the ellipsoid is a calibration-data geometry
estimator, not a separately trained predictive model.
