\title{Mahalanobis Ellipsoidal Steering\\
\large Methodology and Qwen2.5-3B TruthfulQA Protocol}
\author{}
\date{\today}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We describe a Mahalanobis ellipsoidal extension of Spherical Steering for
inference-time control of language-model hidden states.  The method estimates
the center and covariance geometry of calibration activations, whitens a
hidden state, performs a norm-preserving spherical rotation in whitened
coordinates, and maps the result back to the original representation space.
The construction preserves the activation's Mahalanobis radius.  We present
diagonal and low-rank-plus-isotropic covariance representations, a stable
matrix-free intervention, leakage-safe nested evaluation on TruthfulQA, and
the validation procedure used for Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct.  This ellipsoidal method
is an extension of, rather than a method evaluated in, the original Spherical
Steering paper.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation and Scope}

Ordinary activation addition changes both the direction and magnitude of a
hidden representation.  Spherical Steering instead rotates a hidden state on
a Euclidean sphere and preserves its Euclidean norm.  The spherical model,
however, treats the origin as the center of the representation geometry and
all directions as having equal scale.  Transformer activations can be
nonzero-centered, anisotropic, and strongly correlated.  We therefore replace
the Euclidean geometry by a constant Mahalanobis metric estimated from
calibration activations.

Let $\bm h\in\R^d$ be a hidden state, $\bm c\in\R^d$ a calibration center, and
$\bm\Sigma\succ0$ a regularized covariance estimator.  Define
\begin{equation}
  \bm z = \bm W_p(\bm h-\bm c),
  \qquad \bm W_p=\tau^p\bm\Sigma^{-p},\quad
  \tau=\operatorname{tr}(\bm\Sigma)/d,\quad 0\leq p\leq\tfrac12.
\end{equation}
We rotate $\bm z$ toward a truthful prototype in transformed coordinates and map
the result back using $\bm W_p^{-1}$. At $p=0$ this is the identity geometry;
at $p=1/2$ it is normalized full whitening. The trace normalization makes the
map invariant to global covariance scale. The trajectory is a spherical geodesic in
transformed coordinates, equivalently a geodesic under a powered Mahalanobis
metric.  We do \emph{not} claim that it is an ambient-Euclidean geodesic of an
embedded ellipsoid.

\section{Calibration Data and Leakage Control}

For every TruthfulQA question--answer candidate, we concatenate the exact
evaluation prompt and candidate token sequences and extract every hidden
position whose next-token logits score the answer. For prompt length $P$ and
answer length $L$, these are $P-1,\ldots,P+L-2$. Each receives weight $1/L$,
so every answer candidate contributes unit total fitting weight. Each
activation is paired with a binary label $y_i\in\{0,1\}$, where
$1$ denotes a truthful answer and $0$ an incorrect or hallucinated answer.
Activations belonging to the same question are always assigned to the same
split.

For each outer fold, let $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{fit}}$ be the prototype-fitting
set, $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{val}}$ the hyperparameter-validation set, and
$\mathcal D_{\mathrm{test}}$ the untouched test set.  All quantities below---the
center, class means, covariance axes, shrinkage target, retained rank,
prototypes, and radius quantiles---are estimated exclusively from
$\mathcal D_{\mathrm{fit}}$.  Validation and test activations never participate
in geometry fitting.

Our adapted Qwen2.5-3B protocol uses two randomized outer folds with seed 42.
Within each outer development fold, 20\% of questions are reserved for
validation and the remaining 80\% are used to fit prototypes.  Hyperparameters
are selected on validation data and frozen before the outer test fold is
evaluated.  The process is repeated independently for the second outer fold.

\section{Ellipsoid Estimation}

Given fitting activations $\bm x_i$ and reliability weights $w_i$, define the
weighted class means $\bm m_T,\bm m_H$. We independently sweep three steering
origins,
\begin{equation}
 \bm c_{\rm zero}=\bm0,\qquad
 \bm c_{\rm global}=\frac{\sum_iw_i\bm x_i}{\sum_iw_i},\qquad
 \bm c_{\rm midpoint}=\tfrac12(\bm m_T+\bm m_H).
\end{equation}
Weighted covariance uses denominator
$D_w=\sum_iw_i-(\sum_iw_i^2)/(\sum_iw_i)$. For reference, with uniform
weights the means reduce to
\begin{align}
  \bm m_T &= \frac{1}{n_T}\sum_{i:y_i=1}\bm x_i, &
  \bm m_H &= \frac{1}{n_H}\sum_{i:y_i=0}\bm x_i,\\
  \bm c &= \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bm x_i.
\end{align}

\subsection{Covariance residuals}

We support two covariance sources.  Global covariance uses
\begin{equation}
  \bm r_i=\bm x_i-\bm c.
\end{equation}
Pooled within-class covariance uses
\begin{equation}
  \bm r_i =
  \begin{cases}
    \bm x_i-\bm m_T,&y_i=1,\\
    \bm x_i-\bm m_H,&y_i=0.
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
We recommend pooled residuals because the truthful--hallucinated mean
difference does not itself inflate a covariance axis.

\subsection{Shrinkage and flooring}

Let $v_j$ denote an estimated diagonal variance or covariance eigenvalue and
let
\begin{equation}
  \tau=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}v_j.
\end{equation}
With shrinkage coefficient $\gamma\in[0,1]$, we use
\begin{equation}
  v_j^{(\gamma)}=(1-\gamma)v_j+\gamma\tau,
\end{equation}
followed by the relative and absolute floor
\begin{equation}
  v_j\leftarrow\max\{v_j,\epsilon\tau,\epsilon\},
  \qquad \epsilon>0.
\end{equation}
Covariance fitting is performed in float64 where feasible, and saved artifacts
use float32.

\subsection{Diagonal representation}

The diagonal estimator stores only
\begin{equation}
  \bm c\in\R^d,
  \qquad \bm v=\operatorname{diag}(\bm\Sigma)\in\R^d.
\end{equation}
Whitening and coloring are elementwise:
\begin{equation}
  \bm W\bm u=\bm u\oslash\sqrt{\bm v},
  \qquad
  \bm W^{-1}\bm u=\bm u\odot\sqrt{\bm v}.
\end{equation}
This is the lowest-memory estimator, but it ignores correlations between hidden
dimensions.

\subsection{Low-rank plus isotropic residual representation}

The low-rank estimator uses randomized truncated SVD of the fitting residual
matrix and never constructs a dense $d\times d$ covariance or whitening
matrix.  It represents
\begin{equation}
 \bm\Sigma=m U\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_k)\bm U\trans
 +\sigma_\perp^2(\bm I-\bm U\bm U\trans),
\end{equation}
where $\bm U\in\R^{d\times k}$ has orthonormal columns.  Before shrinkage, the
residual variance is estimated by
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_\perp^2=
  \frac{\operatorname{tr}(\widehat{\bm\Sigma})-
  \sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_j}{d-k}.
\end{equation}
The matrix-free whitening operation is
\begin{equation}
\bm W\bm u = \frac{\bm u}{\sqrt{\sigma_\perp^2}}+
\bm U\left[
\left(\bm\lambda^{-1/2}-(\sigma_\perp^2)^{-1/2}\bm 1\right)
\odot(\bm U\trans\bm u)
\right],
\end{equation}
and inverse whitening is
\begin{equation}
\bm W^{-1}\bm u = \sqrt{\sigma_\perp^2}\bm u+
\bm U\left[
\left(\sqrt{\bm\lambda}-\sqrt{\sigma_\perp^2}\bm 1\right)
\odot(\bm U\trans\bm u)
\right].
\end{equation}

\section{Whitened Contrastive Prototypes}

The raw contrastive mean direction is
\begin{equation}
  \bm d=\bm m_T-\bm m_H.
\end{equation}
For ellipsoidal steering, it is essential to whiten this direction before
normalization:
\begin{equation}
  \bm d_z=\bm W\bm d,
  \qquad
  \mTz=\frac{\bm d_z}{\norm{\bm d_z}},
  \qquad
  \mHz=-\mTz.
\end{equation}
Fitting fails explicitly if $\norm{\bm d_z}$ is numerically negligible.  Raw
space spherical prototypes are not reused for ellipsoidal steering.

\section{Adaptive Mahalanobis Steering}

For a hidden state $\bm h$, compute
\begin{equation}
  \bm z=\bm W(\bm h-\bm c),
  \qquad r_M=\norm{\bm z},
  \qquad \widehat{\bm z}=\bm z/r_M.
\end{equation}
Near-zero $r_M$ produces an exact no-op.  Otherwise, calculate directional
similarities
\begin{equation}
  s_T=\widehat{\bm z}\trans\mTz,
  \qquad s_H=\widehat{\bm z}\trans\mHz,
\end{equation}
and vMF-style probabilities
\begin{equation}
  (p_T,p_H)=\operatorname{softmax}(\kappa s_T,\kappa s_H).
\end{equation}
Let $\delta=p_H-p_T$.  Steering triggers only when $\delta>\beta$, with
adaptive strength
\begin{equation}
  t=\operatorname{clip}\left(
  \alpha\frac{\delta-\beta}{1-\beta},0,1\right).
\end{equation}
Here $\kappa$ controls confidence sharpness, $\beta$ controls selectivity, and
$\alpha$ controls the maximum rotation strength.

\subsection{Stable exponential-map Slerp}

Let
\begin{equation}
 q=\operatorname{clip}(\widehat{\bm z}\trans\mTz,-1,1),
 \qquad \theta=\arccos q,
\end{equation}
and define the normalized tangent
\begin{equation}
  \bm v=\frac{\mTz-q\widehat{\bm z}}
  {\norm{\mTz-q\widehat{\bm z}}}.
\end{equation}
The rotated unit direction is
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\bm z}'=cos(t\theta)\widehat{\bm z}
  +\sin(t\theta)\bm v.
\end{equation}
For nearly antipodal vectors or a tiny tangent norm, a deterministic orthogonal
tangent is constructed from the coordinate basis vector least aligned with
$\widehat{\bm z}$.  Already-aligned vectors, invalid parameters, and nonfinite
inputs are handled explicitly.

Finally,
\begin{equation}
  \bm z'=r_M\widehat{\bm z}',
  \qquad
  \bm h'=\bm c+\bm W^{-1}\bm z'.
\end{equation}
Intervention arithmetic uses float32 and the output is returned in the input
activation dtype.

\section{Mahalanobis-Radius Invariance}

Because Slerp preserves unit norm and we restore the original $r_M$,
\begin{align}
  \norm{\bm W(\bm h'-\bm c)}^2
  &=\norm{\bm z'}^2\\
  &=r_M^2\\
  &=\norm{\bm W(\bm h-\bm c)}^2.
\end{align}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
 (\bm h'-\bm c)\trans\bm\Sigma^{-1}(\bm h'-\bm c)
 =
 (\bm h-\bm c)\trans\bm\Sigma^{-1}(\bm h-\bm c),
\end{equation}
up to floating-point tolerance.  Observed maximum relative radius errors in
our experiments were approximately $10^{-7}$.

\section{TruthfulQA Evaluation Protocol}

We use Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct and matched scored-token activations. This retains the paper's
evaluation structure while changing the model size; consequently, it is an
adapted replication and cannot be expected to reproduce the paper's published
7B/8B numbers.

For multiple-choice evaluation, every truthful and incorrect reference answer
is scored by its conditional token log-likelihood.  MC1 indicates whether the
designated best truthful answer outranks all incorrect answers.  MC2 is the
normalized probability mass assigned to truthful answers.  MC3 is the fraction
of truthful references that outrank the highest-scoring incorrect reference.
We also report
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{MC\ Avg.}=\frac{\mathrm{MC1}+\mathrm{MC2}+\mathrm{MC3}}{3}.
\end{equation}

The tuning order is:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item choose candidate layers using permissive gating on validation data;
  \item fit a separate artifact for every layer and covariance configuration;
  \item tune $\beta$ on validation data to calibrate intervention selectivity;
  \item tune $\alpha$ at the selected $\beta$;
  \item optionally tune shrinkage and covariance rank, still using validation;
  \item freeze all choices and evaluate the corresponding outer test fold once.
\end{enumerate}
The second outer fold repeats the complete selection process independently.
No parameter is changed after observing test results.

\subsection{Earlier 25-question smoke tests}

Before introducing the nested paper-style split, we ran 25-question smoke
tests using the legacy fold construction.  The unsteered baseline obtained
MC1/MC2/MC3 of $0.3200/0.4571/0.2119$; spherical steering obtained
$0.4000/0.6314/0.2620$; and the initial diagonal ellipsoid obtained
$0.3200/0.4926/0.2219$.  These runs established end-to-end functionality but
are not pooled with the nested-protocol results because their question split
and tuning procedure differ.

\section{Interpretation and Limitations}

The diagonal estimator is inexpensive but assumes coordinate-wise independence.
At Qwen layer 19 it produced a substantially lower trigger rate and weaker
validation metrics than the rank-128 estimator.  The low-rank representation
captures correlated activation directions and approached spherical validation
performance while obtaining a higher MC1.  This observation is specific to
the current layer, rank, split, and model and is not evidence that low-rank
geometry universally dominates diagonal geometry.

Multiple-choice tuning alone can favor aggressive rotations that harm
open-ended informativeness.  A complete study should additionally evaluate
TruthfulQA TRUE, INFO, and TRUE$\times$INFO on validation data before freezing
hyperparameters.  It should report both outer folds, uncertainty across seeds,
the sensitivity to layer and covariance rank, and a baseline using identical
prompts and splits.  Finally, the ellipsoid is a calibration-data geometry
estimator, not a separately trained predictive model.
